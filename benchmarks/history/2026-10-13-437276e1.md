# Cursor benchmark of the PyAthena 4.1.0 release commit on 2026-10-13

This entry records a bounded large-data run of the [benchmark harness](../README.md) at commit [`437276e1`](https://github.com/pyathena-dev/PyAthena/tree/437276e18c22a3f508c9d436f8e9fa4a0096d439/benchmarks), the commit planned for the PyAthena 4.1.0 release.
The installed distribution reports the version `4.0.1.dev119+g437276e18` because its version is derived from the distance to the latest tag.
It repeats the selection of the [4.0.0 run](2026-10-11-8a69ce03.md) with the changes of #1133: `pandas.read_sql_query()` through SQLAlchemy, a `json` workload, and a smaller set of uninformative cases.
The S3 filesystem comparison of the same commit is a [separate entry](2026-10-13-437276e1-filesystem.md).

## Selection

The run used the bounded selection in [Running on a fleet](../README.md#running-on-a-fleet): 96 jobs over three scales, without warmups.

- **medium**: Cursor and S3FSCursor rows at arraysize 1000 and the unchunked native reads of the DataFrame libraries and Wrangler, flat, three repetitions.
- **large**, flat and nested: Cursor and S3FSCursor rows at arraysize 1000 (five repetitions), Cursor at arraysize 100 (three, one job per repetition), DictCursor flat (three), every native read of the DataFrame libraries and Wrangler (five), the json workload's CSV reads without chunks and with 100,000-row chunks (five), and `read_sql_query()` through the pandas, Arrow, Polars, and S3FS drivers without chunks and with 100,000-row chunks (five) and through the REST driver without chunks (three, one job per repetition).
- **xlarge**: S3FSCursor rows flat, every native read of the DataFrame libraries and Wrangler, the json workload without chunks and with 100,000-row chunks, and `read_sql_query()` through the pandas, Arrow, Polars, and S3FS drivers flat without chunks and with chunks, and through the pandas, Arrow, and Polars drivers nested with chunks only, all with three repetitions.

The configuration matched `config.toml` except for `chunksizes = [10000, 100000, 1000000]` and `timeout_seconds = 5400.0`.
Only the synchronous API ran; the ThreadPool and native asyncio APIs, the initialization suite, and the concurrency suite were left out because the 4.0.0 run measured no difference among the APIs for single queries.

## Run record

Eight Spot hosts started at 13:30 UTC in all four Availability Zones, and the queue `large-1` was published at 13:32 UTC.
Workers used `--api-slots 10 --terminate-host`, and the last job of the queue finished at 16:13 UTC.
Spot interruptions were frequent: 22 job attempts ended early and were continued by later attempts on other hosts, and the 96 jobs finished on 32 different hosts.
A trial that a later attempt repeated successfully appears in the notes as `Superseded: interrupted`.

Six jobs failed after an interruption, all for the same reason: the worker that took over the job could not confirm that the earlier attempt's queries had stopped, because `ListQueryExecutions` was throttled (`ThrottlingException: Rate exceeded`) through its five retries.
Each of those workers then retired its host, so the group shrank to three hosts by 15:42 UTC, and its desired capacity was set back to eight.
After every worker had exited, `cleanup --trials-only --execute` quiesced the trial outputs, and the six jobs ran again under the queue `large-1r` on four hosts from 16:31 to 17:08 UTC, against the same manifest and configuration.
This entry uses the trials of `large-1r` for those six jobs and the trials of `large-1` for the other 90.

The entry's 206 measured cases have 818 successful trials and no failed trial; 15 interrupted trials were superseded, and 8 cases, Wrangler CSV with the nested shape, are unsupported.
The median Athena queue time of every case was at most 119 ms.
No workflow of this repository ran between 13:00 and 17:30 UTC; the daily database sweep had finished at 10:18 UTC.

## Findings

1. **PandasCursor read CSV results faster than Wrangler** (#1132).
   Without chunks, PandasCursor took 10.3 seconds for 1 million flat rows and 74.4 seconds for 10 million, against 12.5 and 95.3 seconds for Wrangler; in the 4.0.0 run it took 13.7 and 110.5 seconds.
   Its client result time at 10 million flat rows fell from 80.9 to 44.4 seconds, and at 10 million nested rows from 164.3 to 86.5 seconds.
   ArrowCursor's CSV client result time fell from 30.4 to 6.5 seconds (flat) and from 67.2 to 16.8 seconds (nested) at 10 million rows.
2. **Chunked reads bounded the memory of UNLOAD results** (#1131).
   At 10 million rows and a chunksize of 10,000, PolarsCursor peaked at 659 MiB (flat) and 642 MiB (nested), against 3,732 and 6,073 MiB in the 4.0.0 run; PandasCursor, which now reads UNLOAD results in chunks, peaked at 604 and 588 MiB, against 5,376 and 19,664 MiB without chunks.
   The chunked reads took longer on the client: PolarsCursor's client result time at 10 million rows rose from 1.2 to 2.2 seconds (flat) and from 3.4 to 6.8 seconds (nested), and PandasCursor's flat time from 1.8 seconds without chunks to 5.1 seconds.
   With 1,000,000-row chunks of 1 million rows, PolarsCursor peaked at 958 MiB (flat) against 501 MiB without chunks.
3. **`dtype_backend="pyarrow"` reduced the memory and time of nested UNLOAD reads in PandasCursor.**
   At 10 million nested rows, PandasCursor with `unload=True` took 37.7 seconds and peaked at 5,897 MiB with pyarrow dtypes, against 86.8 seconds and 19,664 MiB with the default dtypes; Wrangler UNLOAD took 90.8 seconds and peaked at 19,145 MiB.
   For flat rows, the times were the same (18.4 and 19.0 seconds), and the peak fell from 5,376 to 4,886 MiB.
4. **S3FSCursor's memory fell to about half at 1 million rows and to a third at 10 million.**
   Its peak RSS was 324 MiB for 1 million flat rows and 321 MiB for 10 million, against 660 and 940 MiB in the 4.0.0 run, and its total time fell by 7 to 8% (36.7 and 328.5 seconds).
5. **Chunked PandasCursor CSV reads used about 100 MiB more than in the 4.0.0 run.**
   At 10 million flat rows, the peak rose from 256 to 363 MiB at a chunksize of 10,000 and from 332 to 449 MiB at 100,000, consistent with the two 32 MiB blocks of the background cache that the reads now keep.
6. **`pandas.read_sql_query()` through SQLAlchemy depended on the driver by a factor of 33** (#254).
   For 1 million flat rows, the default `awsathena+rest` driver took 230.2 seconds, the GetQueryResults paging of Cursor (226.5 seconds), while `awsathena+arrow` with `unload=true` took 7.0 seconds, `awsathena+polars` with `unload=true` 11.3 seconds, and `awsathena+pandas` with `unload=true` 18.3 seconds; Wrangler took 3.9 seconds through UNLOAD and 12.5 seconds through CSV.
   For 10 million flat rows, `awsathena+arrow` with `unload=true` took 50.1 seconds, against 21.4 seconds for Wrangler UNLOAD and 95.3 seconds for Wrangler CSV.
   `read_sql_query()` builds the DataFrame from the rows of `fetchall()` or `fetchmany()`, so it adds row output to each driver's read: ArrowCursor itself returned the same 10 million rows as a Table in 19.2 seconds, and PandasCursor returned a DataFrame in 19.0 seconds.
   Without chunks, the drivers peaked at 14,796 to 17,218 MiB for 10 million flat rows; with `chunksize=100000`, at 590 to 1,259 MiB, except `awsathena+arrow`, whose cursor reads the whole result before yielding chunks (4,237 to 4,942 MiB).
7. **Without chunks, the json workload used about three times Wrangler's memory in PandasCursor.**
   For 10 million rows, PandasCursor took 337.2 seconds and peaked at 39,679 MiB, against 296.2 seconds and 12,741 MiB for Wrangler; with 100,000-row chunks, 322.0 seconds and 781 MiB against 370.6 seconds and 390 MiB.
   PandasCursor converts json values to Python objects with `json.loads`, and Wrangler returns the column with the `object` dtype; the harness does not compare the two representations.
8. **Cursor and DictCursor paging took 4 to 10% longer than in the 4.0.0 run.**
   Cursor took 226.5 seconds for 1 million flat rows at arraysize 1000 (213.3 in the 4.0.0 run) and 1,062.6 seconds at arraysize 100 (990.6), with the same peak RSS of about 190 MiB.
   The modules of this path (`cursor.py`, `result_set.py`, `converter.py`, and `common.py`) and boto3 did not change between `v4.0.0` and this commit, so the difference did not come from PyAthena's code; the service-side cause was not investigated.

## Limits

- Only the synchronous API ran; asynchronous behavior is inferred from the 4.0.0 run.
- Medium covered a regression subset, DictCursor only large flat rows, and arraysize 100 only Cursor.
- The SQLAlchemy REST driver ran at large only, without chunks; nested xlarge `read_sql_query()` ran with chunks only.
- Three or five trials per case make p95 values descriptive.
- Times include Athena execution and one-second polling. Athena's median execution time of the same nested CSV query at 10 million rows ranged from 136 to 220 seconds between cases, so the total times of those cases differ from the 4.0.0 run by up to 44%; compare client result times there, which stayed within 2.3 seconds of the 4.0.0 run for PolarsCursor and fell for ArrowCursor and PandasCursor (finding 1).
- Comparisons with the 4.0.0 run use the same instance type, snapshots' row counts, and selection, but different hosts, days, and snapshots of the same source partition.
- Peak RSS is sampled every 50 ms and can miss short peaks.
- Results from other instance types or architectures are not directly comparable.
